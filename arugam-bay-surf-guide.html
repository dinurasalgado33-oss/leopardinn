Guide · Arugam Bay

Surfing
Arugam Bay.

A long right-hand point, a season that runs opposite to the rest of the island, and a stretch of coast with more breaks than most people ever get around to.

The season

Arugam Bay works when the rest of Sri Lanka does not. The south-west monsoon that soaks the island’s southern coast blows offshore here, which means the east coast season runs roughly from May through September, peaking around July and August.

Outside that window the wind turns and the swell drops. October through April is quiet — pleasant for everything other than surfing, and a genuinely good time to come if you want the coast without the crowd.

Main Point

The wave that made the place. A long right-hand point break peeling over sand and rock, capable of very long rides when it is working properly.

It is also the most crowded wave on the coast in peak season, and the takeoff is competitive. Best suited to confident intermediates and above. Early morning, before the wind and the crowd arrive, is the session worth setting an alarm for.

Peanut Farm

South of town, and for many people the pick of the coast. Two breaks along the same bay: the main peak is a rangy right that holds size, while the smaller inside section is gentler and forgiving.

That split makes it unusually versatile — a group with mixed ability can all surf here happily, which is rarely true elsewhere on this stretch.

Elephant Rock

A walk up the beach from the villa. A softer, sand-bottomed right that breaks more gently than Main Point, which makes it one of the friendliest waves in the area for improvers.

It is also the most scenic spot on this coast, with the rock itself sitting at the end of the bay. Worth the walk at sunset whether you are surfing or not.

Whiskey Point and Pottuvil

North of Arugam Bay proper. Whiskey Point is a mellow right-hander with a small cluster of places to eat, and it handles beginners well.

Pottuvil Point, further north again, is a long and often near-empty right. Getting there takes a little effort, which is precisely why it stays quiet.

If you are learning

Start at Whiskey Point, Elephant Rock, or the inside at Peanut Farm. Avoid Main Point in peak season until you can confidently hold your own in a crowded lineup — dropping in on a local at a point break is a fast way to have a bad morning.

Boards and lessons are easy to arrange locally and we can point you at people who are good rather than merely available.

When the surf is flat

The lagoon behind the town is worth a morning — crocodiles, elephants at the edges, and a lot of birdlife. Kumana National Park is a reasonable drive south and is one of the better birding parks in the country.

Lahugala, inland, is small but reliable for elephants. And the coast road north towards Pottuvil is a fine ride in its own right.

Common questions

Asked and answered

When is surf season in Arugam Bay?
Roughly May through September, peaking in July and August. The south-west monsoon blows offshore on the east coast, which is why the season runs opposite to southern Sri Lanka.
Is Arugam Bay good for beginners?
Yes, but not at Main Point in peak season. Whiskey Point, Elephant Rock, and the inside section at Peanut Farm are all gentler and well suited to learning.
What is the best wave in Arugam Bay?
Main Point is the most famous — a long right-hand point break — though it gets crowded. Many surfers prefer Peanut Farm for its two breaks and more relaxed lineup.
Is it worth visiting Arugam Bay outside surf season?
If you are not surfing, yes. October to April is quiet and warm, with lagoon trips, Kumana National Park, and an empty coastline.
How far is Arugam Bay from Colombo?
Around seven hours by road. A domestic flight to Batticaloa followed by a drive down the coast is the faster option.
