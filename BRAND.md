# Leopard Inn — Brand Guidelines

_Wilpattu · Arugam Bay_

This is the source document for how Leopard Inn looks, sounds, and presents itself — across the website, social media, print menus, and anything a third party (designer, printer, photographer) might produce on the brand's behalf. A polished, shareable version of this document is also published; this file is the editable source of truth.

---

## 1. Brand story

Leopard Inn began with a simple idea — a room shouldn't separate you from the landscape it sits in. Every villa is raised on a deck, wrapped in glass, and finished in warm timber, with a private plunge pool close enough to reach from the bed.

Two branches, two very different landscapes:
- **Arugam Bay** — the deck looks straight out to the ocean. Built for surf mornings and slow, salty evenings.
- **Wilpattu** — the deck looks out over the scrub jungle that gives Sri Lanka's leopards their name. On the edge of the country's largest national park.

Same handmade teak furniture, same quiet attention to detail — different horizon.

## 2. Audience

Three guest profiles the brand speaks to. Copy and imagery should feel native to at least one of these, never generic "any hotel" messaging:

1. **Surf travelers** — younger, active, come for Arugam Bay's waves and beach life. Speak to mornings, tides, salt, movement.
2. **Wildlife / safari travelers** — come for Wilpattu's leopards and scrub jungle. Speak to stillness, patience, dawn/dusk light, the thrill of a sighting.
3. **Couples / honeymooners** — privacy, plunge pools, quiet evenings. Speak to intimacy and slowing down, not amenities-checklist language.

## 3. Voice & tone

**Warm and literary.** Evocative but understated. The copy should read like a well-written travel piece, not an ad.

- Short, declarative sentences mixed with longer, descriptive ones. Let a single strong image do the work rather than stacking adjectives.
- Second person ("you") used sparingly, for the moments that matter — not in every sentence.
- Never salesy, never exclamation-point-heavy, never "book now!!" urgency language.
- Prefer concrete sensory detail (timber, glass, salt, scrub jungle, golden hour) over abstract superlatives ("luxurious", "world-class", "unforgettable").

**Reference lines from the current site** (this is the calibration — new copy should sit comfortably next to these):
> "Sleep closer to the wild."
> "A room shouldn't separate you from the landscape it sits in."
> "Built for surf mornings and slow, salty evenings."
> "On the edge of leopard country."

**Do:**
- Write like you're describing a real place you love, to a friend who's genuinely deciding where to go.
- Use "villa," not "room" — the whole point of the product is that it isn't a hotel room.
- Let the two branches keep distinct language: Arugam Bay copy leans toward ocean/surf/salt; Wilpattu copy leans toward jungle/stillness/wildlife.

**Don't:**
- Don't use "luxury," "world-class," "unforgettable," "paradise," or "hidden gem" — generic hospitality-marketing filler.
- Don't use emoji in on-site copy or formal materials (WhatsApp replies and social captions are the exception — see below).
- Don't write in the imperative ad-voice ("Book your dream escape today!").

**Where the tone can loosen:** WhatsApp messages and social captions can be warmer/more casual and may use light emoji (🐆 is the one brand-consistent emoji, used sparingly), but should still avoid salesy exclamation-heavy language.

## 4. Logo usage

Three marks: one house crest, two branch crests. All three are drawn to sit on wine (`--wine`) — they are not designed to be placed on white.

- **House crest** (`logo-mark.png`) — nav, footer, favicon, social avatars, any house-level (not branch-specific) placement.
- **Wilpattu crest** (`logo-wilpattu.png`) — Wilpattu branch sections and the Wilpattu menu.
- **Arugam Bay crest** (`logo-arugambay.png`) — Arugam Bay branch sections and the Arugam Bay menu.

**Do:**
- Place the crest on wine, or inside a wine ring/plate when the surrounding background is light.
- Keep clear space of at least half the crest's diameter on every side.
- Use the house crest alone when space is tight — the wordmark ("Leopard Inn") is the first thing to drop, never the crest.

**Don't:**
- Don't recolor the gold linework, or place the crest directly on a photograph without a solid or ringed background behind it.
- Don't pair a branch crest with the house wordmark — branch marks stand on their own.
- Don't stretch, rotate, skew, or add drop-shadow/bevel effects. The crest is always a perfect, flat circle.

**Primary lockup:** crest in a wine ring (52px in the nav) + "Leopard Inn" in Fraunces 600 + "Wilpattu · Arugam Bay" in Karla 600 uppercase beneath it, `letter-spacing: 0.14em`.

## 5. Color

Seven tokens. No ad-hoc hex codes in anything produced for the brand — reference these exact values.

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `wine` | `#3D0F16` | **Primary.** Nav, footer, primary buttons, section grounds. |
| `wine-light` | `#5A1B23` | Wine tint — gradients, lifted wine surfaces. |
| `gold` | `#C9A24E` | **The one accent.** Eyebrows, rules, underlines, crest detail. If something needs to pop, this is the only color that pops it. |
| `ink` | `#241A12` | **Neutral.** Body text on light grounds. |
| `bark` | `#3E2C1E` | **Neutral.** Secondary text, captions. |
| `sand` | `#F6EEDF` | **Neutral.** Alternating section ground, cards on wine. |
| `paper` | `#FBF6EC` | **Neutral.** Default page background. |

Neutrals are a warm family — never substitute pure black, white, or grey. Hairlines use `rgba(36,26,18,0.12)`, not a flat grey rule.

## 6. Typography

- **Fraunces** (serif) — display only: headlines, ledes, the domain/wordmark lockup. Weights 400/500/600, italic reserved for ledes and the wordmark, never body copy.
- **Karla** (sans) — everything else: body copy, nav, buttons, labels, form fields. Weights 400–700.

Full type scale is documented in `.claude/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md` for anyone writing code against it.

## 7. Imagery style

- Warm, natural light — golden hour and soft daylight, not flat studio lighting.
- Real spaces as they are: raised timber decks, glass walls, the plunge pool visible from inside the room. The "pool through the window" shot is a recurring signature image for both branches.
- Favor a slightly candid, lived-in feel over stiff staged real-estate photography — a towel rolled on the bed, a coffee mug on the deck rail, is more on-brand than a sterile empty room.
- Wilpattu imagery should include the scrub jungle / river context, not just the villa in isolation. Arugam Bay imagery should include the ocean/beach context.
- Recurring background texture: a faint dot-rosette pattern (echoing leopard rosettes) used at low opacity behind wine-background sections — this is the brand's one decorative motif. Don't introduce a second decorative pattern.

## 8. Recurring motifs (beyond logo/color/type)

These small devices are what make Leopard Inn material feel like Leopard Inn material, even without the logo present:

- **Gold dot divider** — a small circle-and-scattered-dots glyph used between sections, standing in for a plain horizontal rule.
- **Draw-line underline** — a gold underline that draws itself left-to-right beneath a heading; used to mark the one key phrase in a headline.
- **Rosette-dot texture** — faint repeating dot pattern on wine backgrounds (see §7).

## 9. Quick checklist for anything new

1. Copy sits comfortably next to the reference lines in §3 — warm, literary, no filler words from the "don't" list.
2. Every color used is one of the 7 tokens in §5.
3. Fraunces for display, Karla for everything else (§6).
4. Logo follows the usage rules in §4 — never on a bare photo, never recolored.
5. If it's a photo, it matches §7 — warm light, real spaces, branch-appropriate context.
6. If it needs a decorative flourish, reuse an existing motif from §8 rather than inventing a new one.
