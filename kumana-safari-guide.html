Guide · Kumana

Kumana
National Park.

An hour south of Arugam Bay: a lagoon full of birds, leopards on the tracks, and a fraction of the vehicles you would share a sighting with further west.

What Kumana is

Kumana sits on the south-east coast, at the far end of the same protected belt that contains Yala. For years it was known as Yala East, and plenty of maps still call it that.

Its centrepiece is the villu — a mangrove swamp fed by the Kumbukkan Oya, which floods into a shallow lagoon and draws birds in extraordinary numbers. That lagoon is why the park exists and why most people come.

The birdlife

This is one of the best birding parks in Sri Lanka, and in the right months it is genuinely spectacular. Painted storks, pelicans, spoonbills, herons and egrets nest in the mangroves, and migratory species arrive in numbers through the season.

Even if you did not come as a birder, the density is the sort of thing that converts people. Bring binoculars — far more will happen at distance here than at Wilpattu.

Leopards and elephants

Kumana has both. Leopards are seen on the sand tracks and in the scrub, and elephants are common near the water, particularly in the drier months.

Sightings are less frequent than the southern blocks of Yala, but you will usually have them to yourself, which is most of the appeal.

When to go

The bird season is the thing to plan around. Nesting and migration peak roughly from April through July, which is also when the lagoon is at its most active.

That window happens to line up with the Arugam Bay surf season, so a trip built around waves gets the birds thrown in. The park can close for a period during the driest stretch — worth checking before you fix dates, and we will tell you if you have picked a bad week.

How the day runs

The gate is at Okanda, a drive south along the coast road from Arugam Bay — roughly an hour, and a good drive in its own right.

Morning is the better slot, both for the birds and the light. We book the jeep and the driver-tracker, handle the park tickets, and have you leaving early enough to be at the gate for opening. Drives run about three to four hours.

Kumana or Yala?

If you are staying at Arugam Bay, Kumana is the obvious answer — it is far closer, far quieter, and better for birds.

Yala’s southern blocks have a higher leopard density and better odds on a single visit, but they are a long drive from here and busy enough that a sighting usually means a queue of vehicles. For most guests the trade is worth it.

Common questions

Asked and answered

How far is Kumana National Park from Arugam Bay?
The Okanda entrance is roughly an hour south along the coast road. We arrange the jeep and driver so you reach the gate for opening.
What is the best time to visit Kumana?
Roughly April to July, when nesting and migration peak and the lagoon is busiest. The park can close during the driest stretch, so confirm before fixing dates.
Are there leopards in Kumana?
Yes, along with elephants. Sightings are less frequent than in the southern blocks of Yala, but you will usually have them without a queue of other vehicles.
Is Kumana better than Yala for birds?
For birdlife, yes. The mangrove lagoon at the heart of Kumana draws nesting and migratory species in numbers that few parks in the country match.
How long is a Kumana safari?
A morning or afternoon drive runs about three to four hours. Mornings are better for both the birds and the light.
