# Arugam Bay — photo drop

Put Arugam Bay photos in this folder. Anything here is fair game for the website and for social posts.

**Just drop files in — no prep needed.**

- Any format: JPG, PNG, or iPhone HEIC (HEIC gets converted automatically).
- Straight off the camera or phone is ideal — the bigger the original, the better. Cropping and compressing happens on the way out, so nothing here needs resizing first.
- Filenames don't matter. `IMG_4829.jpg` is fine.

---

## Shot list

Arugam Bay is currently the thin side of the library — Wilpattu has nine strong frames and this branch has essentially none of the property itself. These six shots would close the gap. Roughly in priority order:

**1. The ocean seen from inside.** Shot from the bed or the deck, looking out through the glass to the sea. This is the single most valuable frame — Wilpattu's equivalent (the river through the open wall) has carried nearly every design so far. It proves the brand's whole idea: the room doesn't separate you from the landscape.

**2. Interior with the plunge pool visible through the glass.** Bed in frame on one side, pool showing through the window on the other. Wilpattu's version of this is already a signature image; Arugam Bay needs its own.

**3. The villa from outside, in its setting.** The building among palms or against the sand, ideally low angle. Shows the architecture rather than just the interior.

**4. The plunge pool at golden hour**, with the deck and some ocean or sky behind it.

**5. Surf or beach with something of the property in frame** — a corner of the deck, a railing, a lounger. Without that anchor it reads as generic stock and could be any beach in the world.

**6. Two or three details.** Coffee on the deck rail, folded towels, timber grain, a surfboard against a wall. These fill carousel middles and are the easiest shots to get.

## What makes these work

- **Golden hour.** Early morning or the hour before sunset. Warm low light is the single biggest difference between the frames that look premium and the ones that don't — midday sun and flash are what make a good room photograph badly.
- **Portrait orientation** survives Instagram's 4:5 crop best.
- **Clear the frame first.** Tools, cables, laundry, bins, construction material. One Wilpattu shot had a power drill and work gloves on the deck and had to be cropped hard to be usable.
- **Lamps on, curtains open** for interiors. Mixed warm light plus daylight reads far richer than overhead lighting alone.

## A note on the existing room photos

The current interior shots with the red-and-white bedding read as more basic than the Wilpattu A-frames — but that's the **photography**, not the rooms. They were shot in flat, hard daylight, which flattens the timber and pushes the red to look stark against the white.

The same rooms in warm evening light, lamps on, shot from a lower angle, would read rich rather than plain, and would sit far more comfortably with the wine-and-gold palette. Worth a reshoot before writing them off. If styling is ever an option, swapping the red runner for a neutral linen would do the rest.
